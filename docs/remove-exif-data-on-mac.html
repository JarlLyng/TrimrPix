How to Remove EXIF Data on Mac

Updated June 2026 · by IAMJARL

To remove EXIF data on a Mac: open TrimrPix, drag in your photos, and optimize. EXIF, GPS location, and IPTC metadata are stripped automatically from every image — there is no setting to toggle, and everything runs fully offline on your device.

What is EXIF data?

EXIF (Exchangeable Image File Format) is metadata your camera or phone embeds in every photo. It can include the date and time, camera model and settings, and — most sensitively — the exact GPS coordinates where the photo was taken. Photos also often carry IPTC fields (captions, copyright) and vendor data such as Apple's MakerNote.

Why remove it?

  • Privacy: sharing a photo with intact GPS data can reveal your home, workplace, or a child's school.
  • Smaller files: stripping metadata shaves bytes off every image — useful at scale.
  • Clean publishing: remove camera and editing traces before posting online or sending to a client.

How to remove EXIF data on Mac

  1. Open TrimrPix on your Mac
  2. Drag and drop your photos into the window

That's it. Because removing metadata happens as part of optimization, you also get a smaller file in the same step.

Does removing EXIF reduce image quality?

No. Stripping metadata is lossless — it only deletes the hidden information fields, not a single pixel. TrimrPix does re-encode the image to save space, but at the quality you choose (60–95%), so there is no visible difference for typical use.

Is it private?

Yes. TrimrPix processes everything on your Mac — your photos never leave your device, and the app makes no network requests. See the privacy policy for details.
