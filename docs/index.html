Questions.

Which formats does TrimrPix work with?

JPEG, PNG, GIF, AVIF and HEIC, plus PDFs that are images rather than text, such as scans. WebP files are skipped with a message and left as they were, because macOS has no WebP encoder.

Can it compress PDFs?

Yes, when the PDF is made of images rather than text, as a scan is: each page is re-encoded at the quality you choose. PDFs with selectable text are left alone on purpose, because turning them into images would remove the text and usually make the file bigger, and TrimrPix tells you when it skips one. A PDF whose text was turned into outlines, as some design tools export it, has no text layer either. It is treated like a scan, so it gets smaller, but its text comes out slightly softer.

How does Watch Folder work?

You choose a folder and turn Watch Folder on. When a new image or image PDF lands there, TrimrPix waits until the file has finished copying, optimizes it, and saves the smaller copy beside it. The wait is 2 seconds by default and can be set from 0.5 to 10. It ignores its own output, and it is off until you turn it on.

What happens to my originals?

They are kept. The smaller version is saved next to the original with “-optimized” added to its name, so you can compare them. If you would rather replace the originals, turn on “Overwrite original files” in Settings; it is off by default. If compressing would make a file bigger, TrimrPix keeps the original bytes.

How much smaller will my images get?

It depends on the image and on the size you need. Our sample, 30 iPhone photos (CC0, Wikimedia Commons) at Medium, came out 79 % smaller resized to 2048 px for the web, and 25 % smaller at full size, because iPhones already save their photos compressed. An image that is already well compressed may hardly shrink, and one that would grow is left as it was.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Everything happens on your Mac and your images never leave it. The App Store privacy label reads “Data Not Collected”.

Which macOS do I need?

macOS 15.2 or later.

Can I optimize many images at once?

Yes. Drop in as many as you like and optimize them together or one by one. Watch Folder does it for new images on its own.
